# A Hybrid Cryptographic Cross-Domain Authentication and Rogue UAV Interception Scheme for Low-Altitude Airspace

## Abstract

Addressing security pain points in long-distance cross-management-domain flight scenarios under the low-altitude economy—such as single point of failure in centralized CAs, high authentication latency under massive concurrent UAV requests, trajectory privacy leakage during cross-domain flights, and repeated intrusions by illegal "rogue" UAVs that disguise and whitewash their identities—this paper proposes a hybrid-architecture cross-domain authentication scheme for UAVs integrating lattice-based post-quantum cryptography, BLS short signatures, Sparse Merkle Trees (SMT), IPFS distributed storage, consortium blockchain, and Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKP). First, an elastic inner/outer airspace boundary mechanism is defined to enable early detection of UAV domain-exiting behaviors. Second, a two-phase cross-domain interaction process featuring two-layer pre-authentication and formal authentication is designed, utilizing proxy re-encryption (PRE) for lossless cross-domain ciphertext permission conversion. Blind signatures are employed to generate global anonymous pseudonyms for UAVs to ensure routine trajectory privacy, while chip Physical Unclonable Function (PUF) hardware fingerprints are combined to build a network-wide hardware deadlist. Accompanied by SMT non-membership proofs, malicious UAVs are intercepted during the registration stage, completely resolving the identity whitewashing vulnerability of rogue devices. At the underlying layer, a hierarchical cryptographic system is adopted: the backbone trust link uses NIST-standard Dilithium lattice cryptography to resist quantum attacks; air-to-ground interface communication uses BLS aggregate short signatures to compress transmission overhead; and a modified dynamic accumulator is used for pseudonym blinding and obfuscation, avoiding the network-wide synchronization overhead bottleneck of traditional accumulators. On the simulation front, a heterogeneous air-ground network topology is constructed based on NS-3. The proposed scheme achieves millisecond-level cross-domain verification, supports concurrent access for bulk UAVs, and possesses complete security properties including mutual authentication, unforgeability, strong unlinkability, location privacy, instant revocation, and physical tamper resistance. It is well-suited for long-endurance industry-grade UAV cross-domain control scenarios such as long-distance inspection, logistics delivery, and security patrolling.  

**Keywords**: UAV Cross-Domain Authentication; Low-Altitude Airspace; Blockchain; Lattice Cryptography; Zero-Knowledge Proofs; Rogue UAV Interception; Privacy Protection; Sparse Merkle Tree  

## 1 Introduction

### 1.1 Research Background

With the large-scale development of the low-altitude economy, the holdings of consumer-grade and industry-grade Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) continue to grow. Requests for long-distance flight missions—such as cross-regional inspections, logistics transportation, and all-domain security patrolling—have surged. Consequently, a large number of illegal "rogue flight" behaviors that escape local airspace management and lack airspace approval have emerged, severely disrupting airspace order. Existing airspace management is divided into independent management domains according to administrative and functional boundaries. Legitimate UAVs crossing domains must apply for temporary flight permits from third parties in advance. However, traditional authentication architectures suffer from multiple flaws: centralized Certificate Authorities (CAs) pose single points of failure; multi-domain heterogeneous trust systems are difficult to interoperate; authentication computation and communication overheads surge during massive concurrent UAV accesses; malicious UAVs can disguise themselves as legitimate devices by modifying software identifiers or resetting communication IDs to bypass cross-domain regulation, and can re-register to achieve "identity whitewashing" after being blacklisted; and UAV flight trajectories can be reconstructed by stitching together multi-domain logs, exposing location privacy risks.  

Existing UAV cross-domain authentication schemes mostly rely on a single cryptographic mechanism or a single distributed architecture, making it difficult to simultaneously satisfy post-quantum security, lightweight on-board computation, real-time device revocation, strong privacy protection, and rogue flight interception requirements. Aiming at multi-department (civil aviation, air traffic control, local public security) collaborative airspace regulation scenarios, this paper designs an elastic geographic boundary trigger mechanism, a two-phase cross-domain authentication process, a hardware-fingerprint-level anti-whitewashing system, and a chain-on/chain-off collaborative distributed storage architecture. By hierarchically deploying hybrid cryptographic primitives to balance security and efficiency, this scheme realizes efficient and seamless cross-domain access for legitimate UAVs while accurately capturing and permanently blocking disguised rogue UAVs.  

### 1.2 Core Research Problems

1. **Rigid Airspace Boundary Decision Mechanisms:** Boundary decision mechanisms cannot adapt to dynamic UAV flight speeds and communication latencies, resulting in delayed cross-domain authentication triggering and potential regulation failure after crossing boundaries.  
2. **Long Trust Chains & High Latency in Centralized PKI:** Traditional centralized PKIs involve lengthy cross-domain trust chains with high risks of single points of failure. High authentication latency under massive concurrent UAV accesses fails to meet real-time low-altitude control needs.  
3. **Identity Whitewashing Vulnerabilities in Anonymous Authentication:** Under current anonymous authentication frameworks, blacklisted malicious UAVs can change software identifiers and re-register, presenting identity whitewashing loopholes that lack permanent hardware-level interception mechanisms.  
4. **Inability of Single Cryptosystems to Balance Low Capability & Quantum Risks:** A single cryptosystem cannot simultaneously satisfy low computational capacity on airborne devices and resist future quantum computer attacks. Cross-domain trajectory data interactions easily lead to privacy leakage.  
5. **Time Differences & Overheads in Revocation Synchronization:** Device revocation status synchronization suffers from time lags. Traditional Certificate Revocation List (CRL) storage and retrieval overheads are too large to achieve millisecond-level blacklist verification.

## 2 Related Work

This section reviews literature on cross-domain authentication across UAV networks, VANETs, and IIoT, analyzing their innovations, limitations, and inspirations for our scheme.  

### 2.1 Analysis of Limitations in Existing Schemes

1. **Monolithic Trust Architectures:** Most schemes rely purely on centralized PKI or pure blockchain architectures. The former faces single points of failure, while high-frequency chain interactions in the latter cause consensus delays, failing to balance real-time performance and robustness.  
2. **Single Cryptographic Design:** Using only ECC, bilinear pairings, or chaotic maps lacks a layered hybrid design, making it impossible to satisfy long-term post-quantum security and lightweight airborne communication simultaneously.  
3. **Lack of Rogue Interception Mechanisms:** Existing anonymous authentication relies only on software pseudonym revocation without binding unique hardware features. Malicious devices can re-register and whitewash themselves by replacing firmware or software IDs.  
4. **Missing Boundary Trigger Mechanisms:** Lacking adaptive elastic airspace boundary designs, cross-domain authentication is triggered only after the UAV has completely crossed the boundary, causing regulatory lag.  
5. **Time Lags in Revocation Synchronization:** Relying on CRL/OCSP synchronization introduces long update cycles. Malicious devices can gain unauthorized access during the revocation window due to the lack of local millisecond-level offline verification mechanisms.  

### 2.2 Key Innovations of Our Scheme

1. **Adaptive Elastic Airspace Inner/Outer Boundary Model:** Dynamically calculates safety margins based on UAV speed, heading, positioning error, and wireless communication latency to trigger pre-authentication early, avoiding boundary regulatory lag.  
2. **Two-Phase Layered Cross-Domain Authentication Workflow:** Completes pre-authentication in the boundary buffer zone and formal mutual authentication after physical crossing. Uses proxy re-encryption (PRE) for lossless permission conversion, significantly reducing boundary wait times.  
3. **Hardware Fingerprint-Level Anti-Whitewashing System:** Integrates a network-wide PUF hardware fingerprint deadlist with SMT non-membership proofs to block blacklisted UAVs from re-registering at the physical hardware layer.  
4. **Hierarchical Hybrid Cryptographic Architecture:** Deploys Dilithium lattice cryptography on backbone trust chains for post-quantum safety and BLS aggregate short signatures for air-ground air interface communication to reduce overhead. Modifies dynamic accumulators for pseudonym confusion, eliminating network-wide synchronization bottlenecks.  
5. **Chain-On/Chain-Off Collaborative Distributed Storage:** The consortium blockchain stores only SMT root hashes and IPFS index CIDs, while massive blacklists, tree data, and credential details are stored in IPFS, balancing tamper-proof consensus with storage lightweighting.  
6. **Complete Closed-Loop Security Mechanism:** Combines blind signatures and ZKP for strong routine trajectory privacy, and smart contracts for irreversible network-wide revocation, satisfying all security properties including mutual authentication, anti-MITM, anti-replay, and physical tamper resistance.  

## 3 Preliminaries

This chapter systematically introduces foundational cryptographic, distributed storage, and blockchain theories used in the scheme, covering 11 basic modules: bilinear pairings, dynamic accumulators, extended Chebyshev chaotic maps, lattice cryptography, proxy re-encryption, evaluation criteria, digital signatures, Sparse Merkle Trees (SMT), IPFS, consortium blockchain, and Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKP).  

### 3.1 Bilinear Pairings

Define a bilinear map $e: G_1 \times G_2 \longrightarrow G_T$, where $G_1, G_2$ are additive cyclic groups and $G_T$ is a multiplicative cyclic group with generators $P, Q$, satisfying three core properties: Bilinearity ($\forall a,b\in \mathbb{Z}, e(aP,bQ)=e(P,Q)^{ab}$), Non-degeneracy ($e(P,Q)\neq 1_{G_T}$), and Computability. Implementations use Weil, Tate, or BLS12-381 pairings to derive BLS short signatures. Advantages include short signature lengths and batch verification; disadvantages include higher computational cost than ECC and lack of native post-quantum security. Thus, we use it only as a lightweight auxiliary component for air-ground interface short-term session interactions.  

### 3.2 Dynamic Accumulators

Dynamic RSA multiplicative accumulators require only modular multiplication per iteration with complexity $\mathcal{O}(1)$, enabling rapid generation of unique set member identifiers and native support for membership proofs. Traditional schemes use accumulators for network-wide revocation synchronization, which suffers from a flaw: whenever an accumulator value updates upon device status change, all UAVs must synchronize the latest parameters to generate valid proofs, leading to massive synchronization requests and potential DDoS attacks on ground stations. We prune and modify the accumulator to completely decouple it from revocation synchronization, using it solely for intra-domain pseudonym generation with domain-specific random salt nonces. Revocation is fully offloaded to the SMT + IPFS hardware deadlist.  

### 3.3 Extended Chebyshev Chaotic Map (ECCM)

Generates pseudo-random sequences based on non-linear dynamical equations. It can replace traditional asymmetric cryptography in lightweight key agreement scenarios but suffers from floating-point overflow errors in storage and higher computational consumption. In our scheme, it serves only as a backup lightweight option.  

### 3.4 Lattice Cryptography

Given $m$ linearly independent basis vectors $\mathbf{b}_1,\mathbf{b}_2,\dots,\mathbf{b}_m$, a lattice $\mathcal{L}$ is defined as $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{B})=\{\mathbf{B}\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{x}\in \mathbb{Z}^m\}$. Lattice cryptography belongs to Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC), as Shor's algorithm cannot solve lattice hard problems (e.g., SVP, CVP, LWE/RLWE) in polynomial time. Core operations involve matrix/polynomial additions and multiplications without complex modular exponentiations, making them fit for low-power chips while providing post-quantum protection on backbone trust links.  

### 3.5 Proxy Re-Encryption (PRE)

PRE enables ciphertext transformation across permission domains. A proxy node holds a re-encryption key and transforms a ciphertext decryptable only by domain A into one decryptable by domain B without decrypting the plaintext or learning domain A's private key. We employ bilinear pairing-based unidirectional PRE for inter-domain pre-authentication ciphertext conversion.  

### 3.6 Security and Efficiency Evaluation Criteria

Security metrics include: Mutual Authentication, Unforgeability, Anonymity (using pseudonym $PID$), Unlinkability, Geofence Location Privacy, Forward/Backward Secrecy, Physical Tamper Resistance, and Anti-Replay/Anti-MITM. Efficiency metrics include computational latency and air-interface communication bandwidth consumption.  

### 3.7 Digital Signatures

Includes standard signatures (KeyGen, Sign, Verify under EUF-CMA) and variants: Blind Signatures (for registration privacy) and Ring/Group Signatures. We adopt a layered signature selection: BLS short signatures (48 bytes) for air-ground wireless links and Dilithium lattice signatures for blockchain and inter-domain backbone links.  

### 3.8 Sparse Merkle Tree (SMT)

SMT covers a full 256-bit hash index space with default empty hashes for unassigned leaves. It supports Existence Proofs (membership) and Non-Membership Proofs (proving an index is empty/blacklisted without traversing the entire tree) with complexity $\mathcal{O}(256)$. The full tree is stored in IPFS, while the 32-byte root hash is anchored on-chain.  

### 3.9 InterPlanetary File System (IPFS)

IPFS is a P2P content-addressed storage system using CIDs (Content Identifiers), Merkle DAGs, and DHTs. In our architecture, IPFS acts as the off-chain massive storage vault holding SMT structures, hardware deadlists, and audit logs, resolving blockchain storage explosion problems.  

### 3.10 Blockchain & Smart Contracts

A consortium blockchain among airspace control ground centers serves as a deterministic state machine $S_{t+1} = \Upsilon(S_t, TX)$ using Raft-BFT or dBFT consensus. Smart contracts manage Inter-Domain Authorization and Malicious Device Revocation Auditing.  

### 3.11 Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKP)

Employs Non-Interactive Zero-Knowledge Proofs (NIZK, specifically zk-STARKs/Plonk) to enable UAVs to prove valid credential ownership and SMT non-revocation status without revealing real identities, hardware fingerprints, or Merkle paths.  

## 4 Overall Framework

The overall scheme comprises six core modules, leveraging a consortium blockchain + IPFS dual-layer storage and a layered hybrid cryptosystem.  

### 4.1 System Registration

#### 4.1.1 Domain and Consortium Blockchain Initialization

1. Ground control servers of domains $\mathcal{D}_1,\mathcal{D}_2,\dots,\mathcal{D}_N$ join the consortium blockchain and deploy cross-domain smart contracts.  
2. Each domain generates a lattice master keypair $(pk_{\mathcal{D}_i}, sk_{\mathcal{D}_i}) \leftarrow \text{Lattice.KeyGen}(1^\lambda)$. Domain public keys are broadcast on-chain.  
3. Each domain initializes an independent dynamic accumulator with a domain-specific salt nonce and a 256-level SMT.  

#### 4.1.2 UAV Hardware-Bound Anonymous Registration

1. **Hardware Fingerprint Extraction:** UAV reads its SRAM-PUF 256-bit unique hardware fingerprint $Fingerprint_j$.  
2. **Credential Construction & Blinding:** UAV generates secret $s_j$, forms $M = Real\_ID_j \parallel Fingerprint_j \parallel s_j$, and blinds it: $M' = \text{Blind}(M, r)$.  
3. **Home Domain Blind Signature:** Home domain $\mathcal{D}_A$ verifies device legitimacy and signs $M'$ returning $\sigma'$.  
4. **Unblinding & Global Pseudonym:** UAV unblinds $\sigma'$ to obtain valid signature $\sigma_j$ and computes $PID_j = \text{Hash}(M \parallel \sigma_j)$.  
5. **Off-chain Tree Update & On-chain Anchoring:** $PID_j$ is inserted into $\mathcal{D}_A$'s SMT. The updated SMT is uploaded to IPFS to yield a new CID, and the new root $Root_{\mathcal{D}_A}$ with the CID is locked on-chain via smart contract.  

### 4.2 Boundary Handling & Elastic Inner-Boundaries

An elastic inner/outer double-layer airspace boundary dynamically triggers pre-authentication based on real-time geometric and kinematic calculations.  

#### 4.2.1 Dynamic Safety Margin Calculation

The safety margin $d$ is calculated dynamically as:

$$d = v(t) \cdot \cos(\theta) \cdot \left[ \Delta T_{network}(RSSI) + \Delta T_{PRE} + \Delta T_{P2P} \right] + 3\sigma_{GNSS}$$

  

#### 4.2.2 Exit State Decision Logic

The flight controller runs ray-casting at 20 Hz to compute orthogonal distance $L(t)$ to the boundary. If $L(t) \le d$, it crosses the Inner Boundary, triggering timer $T_{out}$. If $T_{out} > T_{max} = 15.0\mathrm{s}$ without returning to the Core Zone, cross-domain pre-authentication is initiated.  

### 4.3 Inter-Domain Pre-Authentication

1. UAV sends a lightweight request to home ground station using a 48-byte BLS short signature: $Req_{pre} = \{PID_j, \mathcal{D}_B, Timestamp, \mathcal{S}_{ign\_sl}(PID_j \parallel \mathcal{D}_B)\}$.  
2. Home domain server fetches target domain's public key and re-encryption key $rk_{A\to B}$ from the blockchain, converting ciphertext $CT_{\mathcal{D}_A}$ to $CT_{\mathcal{D}_B}$ without revealing plaintext.  
3. Home domain pushes $CT_{\mathcal{D}_B}$ and $PID_j$ via P2P mesh network to target domain RSU, which pre-decrypts and pre-validates the request.  

### 4.4 Formal Cross-Domain Authentication

1. Target base station issues challenge $R_B, TS_1$. UAV responds with $Res_{formal} = \{PID_j, \text{Lattice.Sign}(sk_{uav}, R_B \parallel TS_1), Proof_{smt}\}$.  
2. Target station verifies lattice signature, fetches latest $Root_{\mathcal{D}_A}$ from blockchain, and verifies $Proof_{smt}$.  
3. Upon successful validation, short-term session key $K_{session}$ is negotiated, and $PID_j$ is registered as a valid visitor.  

### 4.5 Trust Update & Malicious Device Revocation

When a UAV exhibits malicious behavior, it is blacklisted locally, broadcast to the smart contract for permanent network-wide revocation, and the home domain updates its SMT (setting $PID_j$ leaf to 0) and uploads the new SMT to IPFS to update $Root_{\mathcal{D}_A}$ on-chain.  

### 4.6 Rogue Device Anti-Whitewashing Interception

When a malicious UAV attempts to re-register using new random seeds or software identifiers, the home CA checks its mandatory SRAM-PUF hardware fingerprint $Fingerprint_j$ against the network-wide hardware deadlist fetched from IPFS. If matched, registration is denied immediately; if not matched, SMT non-membership proofs are further checked to ensure the device has not been previously blacklisted, completely blocking identity whitewashing.  

## 5 System Parameters, Instantiation & Details

### 5.1 Global System Parameters

- $\lambda$: Global security parameter ($\lambda=128$, NIST III level).  
- $q$: Large prime modulus ($q=8380417$ for NTT acceleration).  
- $n$: Polynomial degree ($n=256$, ring $R_q=\mathbb{Z}_q[x]/(x^{256}+1)$).  
- $e, G_1, G_2, G_T$: BLS12-381 pairing curve ($G_1$ outputs 48-byte short signatures).  
- $pk_{\mathcal{D}_i}, sk_{\mathcal{D}_i}$: Lattice master keypair (matrix $k=6, l=5$).  
- $Fingerprint_j$: SRAM-PUF 256-bit hardware fingerprint.  
- $PID_j$: 256-bit global anonymous pseudonym.  
- $Root_{\mathcal{D}_A}$: 32-byte home domain SMT root hash stored on-chain.  

### 5.2 Mathematical Derivations & Cryptographic Instantiations

- **Dilithium3 Lattice Signature:** Public key is matrix $\mathbf{A}$ and vector $\mathbf{t} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{s}_1 + \mathbf{s}_2 \pmod q$. Public key size is 1952 bytes; verification takes < 0.1ms via NTT.  
- **Bilinear Proxy Re-Encryption:** Home initial ciphertext is $CT_{\mathcal{D}_A} = (X = g^r, Y = M \cdot e(h, g^\alpha)^r)$. With re-encryption key $rk_{A\to B} = \beta/\alpha \pmod p$, the transformed ciphertext is $CT_{\mathcal{D}_B} = (\tilde{X} = g^{r\beta/\alpha}, Y)$, decrypted by target domain via $M = Y / e(h, \tilde{X})^\alpha$ without exposing private keys or plaintexts.  

## 6 Simulation Environment

The proposed scheme is implemented and evaluated using NS-3 on Ubuntu 26.06 LTS virtualized under VMware Workstation Pro 17. The network topology models heterogeneous air-ground nodes, evaluating latency, bandwidth, and concurrent access capacity.  

## 7 Conclusion and Future Work

This paper presents a hybrid cryptographic cross-domain authentication scheme for UAVs in low-altitude airspace. By combining lattice cryptography, BLS short signatures, SMT, IPFS, consortium blockchain, and ZKP, the scheme provides elastic boundary pre-authentication, hardware-fingerprint rogue interception, trajectory privacy preservation, and millisecond-level revocation. Future work includes expanding performance benchmarks under ultra-large-scale UAV swarms and integrating legacy aviation PKI protocols.
